# Ava · production line for a face-led persona

Companion to `config/personas/ava-beauty.yaml`. That file says who she is;
this one says how the studio makes the same face, voice and life hold across
every post, and what has to be built before the first one.

Decisions this rests on (operator, 2026-09-04): English and global, no
subtitles · a daily life — car, Pilates, dinner — with one recurring friend ·
an original face, never a real person · Seedance 2.0 at 1080p for scenes,
Veo 3.1 for spoken shots · a designed voice · 70% story / 30% product,
sponsored only past 25k followers · Instagram, YouTube Shorts, TikTok ·
1080p and the Veo share approved.

## 1. The identity file

The identity file is the asset everything else references. It is built once,
versioned with `style_version`, and regenerated only on purpose.

1. **Candidates.** 60–100 portrait candidates from the `character` block —
   FLUX.2 [pro] and Seedream 4.5, several seeds, three lighting setups
   (golden window, overcast, tungsten evening). Budget about $5–10 on fal.
   The operator picks ONE. Nothing in this step may reference a real person's
   photo or name.
2. **Reference set.** From the chosen face, 40–60 frames: nine head angles,
   six expressions, three lighting setups, hair in her three states (low
   bun, down, high ponytail), the earrings always present. Generated by
   image-to-image from the canonical portrait, judged for identity before
   they join the set.
3. **Trained identity.** A LoRA on fal (FLUX LoRA fast training, a few
   dollars) from the reference set. Used for every still. It is the one
   thing that makes a Tuesday post look like a Monday post.
4. **Reference bundles.** Seedance 2.0 takes up to 9 image, 3 video and 3
   audio references per generation; Veo 3.1 takes 3 images. Two fixed
   bundles are cut from the reference set and never improvised per post.
5. **Identity regression.** The judge (`studio/factory.py: judge_pick`)
   gets an extra criterion from `visual_grammar.identity_lock`: compare to
   the canonical portrait; a different face is a rejection whatever its
   beauty. The regression is run on every candidate, not sampled.

Noor, the best friend, gets the same treatment at smaller scale: 30
references, her own bundle, her own regression. No third face in phase 1.

## 2. Stills

Every still — carousel slide, cover frame, first frame of a video — renders
against the LoRA plus the image bundle: Nano Banana Pro when a real product
or a legible object is in frame (it holds packaging and text), FLUX.2 [pro]
otherwise. The scene comes from `recurring_world`; the hour comes from the
release window the post is bound to.

## 3. Video routing

| Does she speak on camera? | Model | Why | List price |
|---|---|---|---|
| No — a scene, music bed | Seedance 2.0, 1080p, reference bundle | best identity hold across shots, native ambient audio | $0.682/s (fal) |
| Yes — a line to camera | Veo 3.1, 1080p, three references | native speech and lip-sync in one pass | $0.40/s |
| Fallback for a spoken line | Seedance clip + Kling 3.0 lip-sync on the designed voice | when Veo's face drifts | Kling per-second + lip-sync |

A 20-second short is two or three clips of 4–15 s each, cut on a beat. The
retake multiplier in the model is 1.5: a third of clips fail identity or
hands and are re-shot. The first frame of every clip is a still from §2, so
the face enters the video already locked.

## 4. Voice

One voice, designed in ElevenLabs (voice design, not a clone of anyone),
locked by voice id and versioned with the identity file. Eleven v3 at $0.10
per 1,000 characters — a spoken short is about 15 seconds of speech. Noor's
voice is designed the same way. Spoken lines are written by the brain in the
`voice` register and pass the caption linter before they are recorded.

## 5. Product placement

Products are real; the face is not. That order sets the rules.

- The brand's own product photography is the only source for packaging.
  Product insertion is an edit step (Nano Banana Pro or FLUX.2 edit) on a
  finished still, then image-to-video; the judge checks the packaging is
  legible and exact.
- A product appears as something she is doing — the sunscreen in the car,
  the cleanser after Pilates, the lipstick before dinner. `grwm_short` is the
  home of product moments; `story_short` may show one in passing.
- Months 1–3: products she "chooses" (no brand relationship), affiliate links
  from month 2. Sponsored posts only past 25,000 followers, and every one of
  them carries the paid-partnership tag and a plain "#ad" or "paid
  partnership" line on top of the AI disclosure (`claims.sponsored_rule`).
- Claims: the linter's banned list in the persona file, plus a judge check
  for on-screen text. Cosmetic effect yes; medical effect never.

## 6. Time and release

Ava lives in `America/Los_Angeles`; the operator lives ten hours ahead. The
line already runs asynchronously (harvest at 04:00 and 13:00 UTC, drafting
once a day, approval from the console); one feature is missing:

**Scheduled release.** `approve()` publishes immediately today. The change:
an approved draft is bound to the next release window for its scene
(`content.release_windows`), the guard's jitter is applied inside the
window, and the publisher fires at that time. A morning scene approved at
16:00 Turkey time publishes at 07:00–09:00 in Los Angeles that evening; an
evening scene approved at night publishes at 18:30–21:00 LA time, which is
04:30–07:00 in Turkey, unattended. The story hour and the audience hour are
the same hour by construction.

## 7. Platforms

- **Instagram** — Reels and carousels through the existing Graph API leg,
  in approve mode. Primary surface.
- **YouTube Shorts** — the same asset through the existing YouTube publisher,
  Shorts metadata added.
- **TikTok** — new leg: the Content Posting API requires an approved app
  and a review; drafts publish in approve mode like the others. Until the
  leg exists the operator posts by hand from the console's export.
- Cross-posting is one asset, three uploads; captions vary slightly per
  platform (the linter runs on each).

## 8. Compliance, in one place

- AI disclosure in the bio, in every caption (mechanical `post_disclosure`)
  and through each platform's AI-content label. Non-negotiable; the FTC and
  the platforms require it for realistic synthetic people.
- Paid content: platform paid-partnership tag plus a plain-language line.
- Cosmetics claims: cosmetic effect only; the banned list in the persona
  file is the floor, not the ceiling.
- No real person's likeness at any step; no minors in any scene; alcohol is
  never the subject of a scene; no sexualised framing — brand safety is the
  revenue line.
- The car and locations stay generic until a brand relationship says
  otherwise.

## 9. Cost of a published short at these settings

From the memorandum's model (section 05) with 1080p and the Veo share:
about 20 s of video per short, blended $0.60–0.68 per generated second,
retake 1.5, a cover, a music bed, 15 s of voice on spoken shorts, a 10%
buffer, and today's 34% editor acceptance — roughly **$45–60 per published
short**, $1.50 per carousel. Acceptance is the lever the identity file is
expected to move: a locked face rejects for taste, not for drift.

## 10. First ninety days

- **Days 0–14 — identity.** Candidates, the operator's pick, the reference
  set, the LoRA, both bundles, the voice; Noor's smaller set. Format
  definitions for `story-short`, `talk-short`, `grwm-short`.
- **Days 15–30 — the line.** Scheduled release windows; TikTok leg started;
  claims linter extended; cost per short recorded in the ledger. First
  unattended week of drafts, none published.
- **Days 31–60 — cadence.** One short a day and three carousels a week,
  released from the console into the LA windows. Report #1 with measured
  cost per short and identity rejection rate.
- **Days 61–90 — learn.** Format learning on saves and shares; affiliate
  links live; first creator-program applications where thresholds allow.
  Gate 1.

## 10b. A side effect to know about

`studio/persona.py` picks the default persona by sorted id when neither
`--persona` nor `PERSONA` is given, and `ava-beauty` now sorts first. The
cloud routine always passes `--persona`, so production is unaffected; a bare
`python run.py` on a laptop now speaks as Ava until she has accounts, at
which point the guard gates it. Set `PERSONA=june` in a local `.env` to keep
the old default, or pass the flag.
